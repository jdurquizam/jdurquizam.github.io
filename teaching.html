Teaching & Mentorship

As an Associate Professor at the Universidad Nacional de la Amazonía Peruana (UNAP), I integrate my field research directly into the classroom. My teaching philosophy centers on providing students with hands-on experience, bridging basic theoretical ecology with advanced technological applications to study the Amazon basin.

Courses I Teach

Ecology of Amazonian Ecosystems

An exploration of ecosystem functioning, biodiversity, and carbon exchange dynamics, with a specific focus on the unique ecological drivers of the Peruvian Amazon.

Ecological Modeling

Teaching methodologies to predict forest dynamics, simulate disturbance impacts like windthrows, and understand climate-ecosystem feedbacks.

Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Remote Sensing

Practical application of spatial analysis tools to identify patterns of forest recovery, map disturbances, and manage large-scale ecological datasets. Training students in multi-spectral image analysis using platforms like Google Earth Engine, Landsat, and Sentinel-2 to monitor vegetation indices and track deforestation.

Data Science in Ecology

Applying programming languages (such as R and Python) and machine learning techniques to analyze large ecological datasets, climate variables, and earth observation data.

Space and time forest attributes research gruop (VETAF)

In addition to standard coursework, I lead a Space and time forest attributes research gruop at UNAP. Here, I mentor students in practical, field-based science. We collaborate on projects ranging from establishing forest inventory plots in windthrown regions to soil sampling and the deployment of sensor networks. This hands-on involvement allows students to directly contribute to broader regional initiatives, such as the PanAmazoniaFlux and AndesFlux research networks.
